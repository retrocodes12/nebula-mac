Nebula for Mac and iPhone — native Swift apps (SwiftUI, libmpv engine). macOS 13 or later on Apple silicon and Intel; iOS 16 or later, iPad included.

**New in 0.4.0** — catching up with Nebula on the TV, Android and the browser. Series skip their recap and opening titles: a Skip button while they play, or Auto in Settings, and the next episode is offered as soon as the credits start. Bring your own seekr.tv key (Settings › Playback) and pictures follow the pointer, or your finger, along the scrubber; the key follows your profile to your other screens. Subtitles get a style — size, colour, background, edge, font, position, bold — in the player's Subtitles panel and in Settings, shared with your TV and phone, and a timing nudge for a file that runs early or late. A title page shows its Universe: what it follows, what follows it, its spin-offs and remakes. The streams page names the add-ons it is still waiting for. Live sports rows that need their own request headers now play — including streams whose host hides its video inside pictures — and the sports add-on's "India only" rows are offered here too. Supporters see their tier and mark, can choose a mark from Supporter Plus up, put their name on the wall, redeem a code, manage a monthly plan and pick Gold, Ice or Mint; and anyone can open the support page from Settings.

**New in 0.3.3** — a smoothness and polish pass on both apps. The pages under the player no longer redraw while a film plays, posters and backdrops are prepared before they are drawn and no longer flash a placeholder when you come back to them, a long list of streams scrolls smoothly, and the art on Home only moves on while you are looking at Home. On the iPhone the player turns to landscape and keeps its controls clear of the Dynamic Island and the home indicator, a swipe in from the left edge goes back a page, messages show over the player, a keyboard on an iPad plays, pauses, skips and closes the player, and on a phone with a 120 Hz screen the app's animations can use its full rate. On the Mac, Command-[ and Esc go back from what is on screen — over a film they close its menu or the film, never the page underneath — Command-F opens Search ready to type, a held key no longer flips a film between playing and paused, Space plays a finished film again from the start, and the previous and next track keys skip back and forward. Episode dates are no longer a day early west of UTC, the facts under a title never end a line on a dot, a title an add-on lists twice shows once, an empty streams page offers a way to Add-ons, text on an iPad keeps to a readable width, and the update check asks Nebula's own releases list first.

**New in 0.3.2** — a fix pass on both apps. Sync no longer loses a change another device made a moment earlier, an add-on synced from another device can no longer be dropped by a change made here, and an add-on can no longer be installed twice. Closing the player is final: a slow Next episode or a slow stream can no longer reopen it or stop the next film. A live stream that ends offers Try again. Subtitles: Off and your language now apply to a file's own tracks, the same language is recognised whatever code it comes with, and a choice you make in the player is not overridden by subtitles that arrive later. Protected streams find their licence in more manifests, picture-quality limits work on every manifest, catalog pages that failed to load can be retried, and a broken add-on reply is shown as an error.

**New in 0.3.1** — a security update. An add-on, or a Nebula link opened from a web page, can only hand the player a web address (never a file on this computer), and on the iPhone your sign-in is kept out of backups, so restoring a backup onto another phone does not make it this one.

**New in 0.3.0** — a full audit pass on both apps. The Mac answers its play/pause keys, headphone controls and Control Center, and the phone's lock screen shows what is playing. Home no longer waits for a slow add-on and never reshuffles the rows you are looking at; add-ons that could not be reached are asked again when the connection comes back, and a streams page says how many did not answer. Subtitles attach once both the video and your add-ons are ready. A film you seek back into after its end plays on instead of starting over, the phone stays awake while a stream is starting, text follows the phone's text size, the title art runs under the status bar, and small buttons take a full-size tap. Progress that reaches the app broken can no longer wipe a resume point.

**Install on a Mac**: open `Nebula.dmg`, drag Nebula to Applications. The app is not notarised, so the first time macOS refuses it: go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and choose **Open Anyway** beside the line about Nebula (on macOS 13 and 14, right-click › **Open** also works).

**Install on an iPhone**: `Nebula.ipa` is unsigned — there is no Apple developer account behind this — so you sign it with your own free Apple ID using AltStore, SideStore or Sideloadly from a computer, then trust it in Settings › General › VPN & Device Management. Free signing lasts **seven days** at a time; AltStore and SideStore renew it for you. The README has the details.

In this version: add-ons and their catalogs, search and Discover, title pages with seasons and episodes and their Universe, streams with badges, the player (every common format, protected streams and streams that need their own headers, subtitles from add-ons with your style and a timing nudge, audio and subtitle menus, speed, skip intro and recap, Seekr scrub pictures, next episode), Continue Watching, My List, mark as watched, Support Nebula, and a Nebula profile that syncs add-ons, progress, My List, your subtitle style and your Seekr key with your TV, phone and browser.

Live streams that come in several qualities start in a few seconds: the app reads the stream's manifest once and hands the player one quality (Settings › Playback › Picture quality picks which).

The phone app is the same app: the same add-on client, the same engine, the same profile and sync, with a tab bar instead of a sidebar and a player built for a finger — tap to wake the controls, double tap either side to skip, drag the scrubber.

These versions were built and checked entirely on build machines — they compile, play a plain file, an HLS playlist, protected DASH and a live protected sports stream there, and every screen was looked at in screenshots. Neither has yet been run on a Mac or a phone on someone's desk. If something is off on yours, please open an issue.
